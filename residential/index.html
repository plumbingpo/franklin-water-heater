The Water Heater Team Homeowners Rely On

A typical household runs through a surprising amount of hot water every day, so a struggling water heater throws off everything from morning showers to the evening dishes. Left alone, a heavy layer of sediment or a weeping relief valve can turn an inconvenience into a flooded utility room. When something goes wrong, you need a team that shows up fast, pins down the fault accurately, and fixes it right the first time. That's exactly what Franklin Water Heater delivers.

Our trucks carry the most common failure parts, including elements, thermostats, thermocouples, and relief valves, so most routine water heater repairs are finished on the first visit, whether the heater is a gas tank, an electric tank, a tankless model, or a hybrid heat pump.

  • Leaking tanks, valves, and fittings
  • Gas, electric, and heat pump units
  • Water heater repair and replacement
  • Annual flushing and anode rod checks
  • Tankless water heater installs and service

No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Nobody likes a surprise on the invoice. That's why Franklin Water Heater quotes every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the elements or burner, the anode rod and relief valve, and the venting and supply lines. You'll know what's wrong, what the fix involves, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment settles on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod slowly dissolves, and the tank lining starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, call for an inspection right away. An early diagnosis often keeps a fixable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

Not every call is an emergency. Many homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with regular descaling), replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Maintenance Plans

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Each maintenance visit from Franklin Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and sets a safe water temperature. Minor faults are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the heater stays running clean and efficient.

Many homes do best with one flush a year, and houses on hard water may need two. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household works it, and local water quality, which controls how quickly sediment builds up and how long an anode rod lasts.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule helps. A clean tank heats water faster with less noise, a healthy anode rod keeps rust from attacking the steel, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Proper Sizing

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Getting the size right is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before recommending a new heater, Franklin Water Heater works through the details that determine your household's peak demand: how many people and bathrooms you have, back-to-back showers, laundry and dishwasher timing, and your incoming water temperature. That gives you a unit with enough first-hour capacity or GPM to cover your busiest hour without paying to heat water you'll never use.

Needs change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can overwhelm the tank that once kept up just fine. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while heat pump water heaters draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Frequent Failures

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Franklin

Homes in Franklin run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Franklin Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with an expansion tank plus a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before they spread. We also check the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
