Tankless units are clever machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater springs into action only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Franklin, TN homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Franklin Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Franklin, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. Error codes narrow the search, but they rarely name the broken part outright. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

We work on tankless heaters from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Takagi, and the other major brands, gas-fired and electric alike. The fix depends on the diagnosis: a full descaling flush through the isolation valves when scale is choking the heat exchanger, a new flow sensor when the unit no longer notices a faucet opening, a fresh igniter or cleaned flame rod when ignition keeps failing, or a vent correction when the unit trips on combustion air. Sometimes the answer is bigger. A gas line undersized for the unit's full firing rate has to be upsized, and a heat exchanger cracked by years of scale may make replacement the smarter call on an older unit. When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Repair

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A fault code on the front panel and no hot water
  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running
  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • Unusual noises from the cabinet, like a whine, a roar, or a rattle
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up

The Benefits of Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair

There are two reasons tankless repair belongs with a professional: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. A mistake with either one is not a small mistake.

A trained technician with the right instruments gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. We descale with a circulating pump and the right solution, then flush thoroughly so nothing corrosive is left in your plumbing. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, you will hear it from us before you spend money on a repair.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
