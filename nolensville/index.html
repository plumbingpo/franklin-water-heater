Keeping Your Nolensville Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Typical Water Heater Issues in Nolensville

Long before a tank floods a Nolensville garage, it almost always shows warning signs. Look out for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or showers that run cold sooner every month. Those point to sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it needs a professional look soon. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
