What makes a water heater installation different from a replacement? Mostly, the new work. Adding a heater for an addition, relocating one out of a living space, upgrading to a larger tank or a tankless unit, or converting between gas, electric, and heat pump all require piping, venting, or wiring that did not exist before. In Franklin, TN, that work also has to meet local code, which can mean permits, drain pans, expansion tanks, and seismic straps depending on the location. Franklin Water Heater plans the whole job up front so the heater performs, the installation passes, and nothing gets improvised on the day.

Every installation starts with sizing and location. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Location is the next question. Gas units need a way to vent outdoors and enough air for combustion. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. Electric tankless units can need a heavy dedicated circuit and the panel capacity behind it. Above finished space or in an attic, the heater needs a drain pan piped somewhere safe. We talk it all through in plain language and then give you a firm price.

On installation day, every step gets verified. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. Electric and heat pump units get a circuit matched to their load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Then we fill the heater, confirm draft and combustion on gas models, and set a safe temperature. Ready to start? Call (615) 237-6853 for an assessment in Franklin.

Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Venting, relief valve piping, or gas connections that do not look right
  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up
  • A water heater installed above finished rooms with no drain pan
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Remodel plans that add fixtures or a second bathroom
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered
  • Interest in tankless or heat pump efficiency

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Start with the gas line. A new heater on an undersized line starves for fuel whenever the furnace or range is running, and a whole-house tankless unit often needs a larger line than the house already has. Vent material, size, pitch, and clearances all have to be right, or exhaust drifts back inside. Electric models bring their own math, since breaker, wire gauge, and panel capacity all have to match. Add relief valve piping, dielectric fittings, expansion tanks, drain pans, seismic straps, and permit rules that vary from one jurisdiction to the next, and there are a lot of details to get right.

What you get from a pro is an installation that is done once. Every joint is tested, every gas install is checked for draft and carbon monoxide, and the temperature is set to protect against scalding. Rushed installs tend to leave undersized gas lines, bad vent pitch, and missing expansion tanks behind. Ours are done to code and to last. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
