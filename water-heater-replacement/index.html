Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Replacement?

Start with the venting. Gas water heater exhaust carries carbon monoxide, so an undersized, badly sloped, or loose vent can spill it back into living space. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and the details pile up quickly. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code, and some jurisdictions require a permit. Switching to tankless or a heat pump adds new gas line sizing, vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines to the list.

Professional replacement gets all of it right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your household, verify the gas line, vent, and electrical service, and install whatever code items apply. Before leaving, we leak-test, verify draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature. It is what separates a heater that runs quietly for years from one that has problems from the start. And if a repair would buy you several good years on your current tank, we tell you that honestly instead of selling you a new one.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
